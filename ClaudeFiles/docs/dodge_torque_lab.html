Dodge Torque Lab

A side-view simulation of what a dodge does to a ball it is touching: the car's spin, its horizontal dodge kick, RocketSim's extra car-ball impulse, and surface friction, each shown separately so you can see which one sets the launch. Constants are read from this repo's RocketSim source.

Reading the model

  • The launch direction comes from the CM-to-ball line, not the contact normal. RocketSim builds the extra impulse from the vector between the car's center of mass and the ball's center, squashes its vertical part to 35%, and removes 35% of its forward component. At the default setup the ball is 45° from straight up as seen from the center of mass, and that impulse points about 28° above horizontal, not 45°.
  • The car's spin is not constant, and it is small during a short contact. The dodge adds 224 rad/s² (260 for a side flip) every tick for 0.65 s, so the car's spin ramps from zero to the 5.5 rad/s cap over three ticks, 1.9 and 3.7 rad/s on the first two. At the defaults the extra impulse separates the ball after two ticks, so the car's spin never gets past 3.7 rad/s while touching and changes the result very little. When the car keeps pushing, the ball also pushes back on the car: with the nose at −135°, underside contact, front flip, offset at the nose end, the contact first reverses the car's spin for several ticks, and the spin that finally builds raises ball speed from about 540 to 1220 uu/s.
  • Ball spin comes from friction on the sliding face. With the car's 500 uu/s kick sliding along a tilted underside, the high contact friction (2.0) grips and spins the ball at about 2.8 rad/s even with the torque switched off. Set friction to 0 and the spin goes to zero.
  • Moving the contact point shifts the result predictably. At the default setup, sliding the contact from the tail end to the nose end of the underside (back flip) drops ball speed from about 680 to 455 uu/s and lifts the launch angle from 22° to 47°.
  • The kick follows the nose's horizontal direction, so past ±90° of nose pitch a back flip kicks forward. That is the musty flick orientation, and it is the default here (nose at 135°). There the car's speed along its nose is negative, so RocketSim uses the plain 500 uu/s with no speed scaling. A front flip in the same orientation gets the large backward-dodge scaling instead, up to about 1330 uu/s directed backward.
  • When in the dodge the ball is touched matters through four things. The car's spin is 3.7 rad/s at 25 ms and at the 5.5 cap from about 40 ms. With the contact geometry held fixed, the gain from spin stays flat from about 25 ms until roughly 510 to 620 ms and then collapses, because once the torque stops (650 ms) the ball drains the car's spin with nothing to restore it. The car also keeps rotating at about 315° per second, so every 10 ms of delay turns the nose another 3°, which steers the launch angle. In the musty setup with a 500 uu/s approach, starting the flip 25 ms before contact launched the ball at 35° and 1290 uu/s, and 400 ms before launched it at 92° and 880 uu/s. Finally, the kick and the approach move the ball relative to the car while the car rotates.
  • Musty setup. In this model the roof reaches a ball that is above and ahead of the car only when the nose starts pitched down past −90°: the back flip then turns the roof through forward-facing and up. With the nose pitched back past +90° the wheels face that ball instead. At the default (nose −100°, ball 100 uu above the center of mass and 150 uu ahead, gravity on) the roof touches the ball 133 ms into the flip, with the car's spin at the 5.5 cap and the nose turned 51°, and the ball leaves at about 1260 uu/s and 41°. A ball 50 uu above the center of mass is hit sooner (50 ms) and faster (about 1390 uu/s at 23°). At 150 uu above it the roof barely reaches it, around 280 ms and slow, or misses. A ball less than about 100 uu ahead is touched at the tail edge within 8 ms, before the car has any spin, and leaves at 610 to 870 uu/s. If the ball is rising 100 uu/s relative to the car at the flip start, speed drops to about 630 uu/s.
  • Repeated extra impulses compound. RocketSim reapplies the extra impulse every second tick while contact persists, using the current relative speed. A ball held against a moving face can gain speed geometrically. That is the rule as written in Arena.cpp, and this page follows it.

Model and limits

Planar (side view, no roll or yaw dodges). Gravity is off by default, since both bodies fall together; the gravity toggle applies 650 uu/s² to both and RocketSim's damping of the car's fall from 0.15 s into the flip. Launch speed and angle are read at the end of the last contact tick. The ball is at rest relative to the car before contact unless a closing speed or relative speed is set. Contact is solved per 120 Hz tick with sequential impulses, friction cone 2.0, restitution 0, like RocketSim's car-ball override. Octane hitbox 120.5 × 38.7 uu offset (13.9, 20.8) from the center of mass, car mass 180, ball mass 30 with I = 0.4·m·r², car inertia from the box. Extra impulse follows Arena::_BtCallback_OnCarBallCollision; dodge constants follow Car::_UpdateAirTorque and RLConst.h, which match RLUtilities' dodge model (torque added to angular velocity each tick for 0.65 s, cap 5.5 rad/s). After the flip, air damping of 30 × 0.0959 per second pulls the spin back toward zero. This is a re-implementation, not RocketSim itself, and has not been checked against RocketSim runs because the collision meshes are not in the repo. Treat sizes of effects as estimates and directions as reliable.
